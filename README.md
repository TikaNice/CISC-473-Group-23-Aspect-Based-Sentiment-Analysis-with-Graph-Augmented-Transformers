# CISC 473 Group 23 — Aspect-Based Sentiment Analysis with Graph-Augmented Transformers

Three-class aspect-term polarity classification (negative / neutral / positive) on
SemEval-2014 Restaurant and Laptop. Two models are compared under one protocol:

- **BERT** — `bert-base-uncased` on the sentence–aspect pair, `[CLS]` pooled.
- **BERT+GCN** — the same encoder with word-level dependency-graph message passing, two
  GCN layers, aspect-node pooling, concatenated with `[CLS]`.

Experiments cover full data, three reduced training budgets, Restaurant→Laptop zero-shot
transfer, and a graph control that replaces dependency edges with self-loops only.

## Experiment status

- In-domain grid: 2 domains × 2 models × 4 budgets × 3 seeds = 48 runs.
- Transfer: 6 zero-shot evaluations from the Restaurant full-data checkpoints.
- Graph control: 2 domains × 2 budgets × 3 seeds = 12 identity-adjacency runs.
- Extension controls: 12 constituency-adjacency runs and 12 no-graph aspect-pooling runs on the same
  domains, budgets and seeds. Their table is [results/reports/extension_summary.md](results/reports/extension_summary.md).
- The optional 1/2/4-epoch BERT runs are outside the final comparison.

The final 60 training runs, six transfer evaluations and 24 extension runs used `cuda:0`. Result records identify the seed and device. Training records also identify the PyTorch build. The shared split and budget protocol
is documented in `IMPLEMENTATION.md`. Transfer records link to the source checkpoint. `results/reports/experiment_status.csv` lists complete and pending runs;
`results/INDEX.md` indexes every saved record.

## Implementation scope

Both models start independently from `bert-base-uncased` and are fine-tuned end to end.
The GCN uses one dependency graph per example, aspect pooling and `[CLS]` concatenation.
The pooling control removes the graph layers and keeps the same aspect pooling, so it is a
second baseline trained under the project protocol.
It is not the ASGCN, DualGCN or BERT-PT author implementation, and published numbers in
the report are reference values under the authors' own protocols rather than
matched-protocol reproductions.
